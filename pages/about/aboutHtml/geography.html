A Country of Many Stories

From historic northern towns and forested mountains to rose-red deserts and the Red Sea, explore the character of Jordan’s twelve governorates.

01 / 12 · Northern Jordan

Irbid

Irbid brings together ancient cities, fertile countryside, university culture and lively northern markets.

IN THE PHOTO · Dar As-Saraya Museum · Irbid

Dar As-Saraya Museum occupies a historic Ottoman-era building in central Irbid. Its displays introduce the archaeological heritage of northern Jordan, making it a fitting starting point before exploring Umm Qais, local museums and the surrounding green valleys.

02 / 12 · Northern Highlands

Ajloun

Ajloun is known for its lush hills, medieval fortress, village traditions and woodland trails.

IN THE PHOTO · Mar Elias · Ajloun Highlands

The hilltop archaeological site of Mar Elias is associated with the Prophet Elijah and contains remains of early Byzantine churches. Set among the highlands northwest of Ajloun, it combines religious heritage, old stonework and peaceful countryside views.

03 / 12 · Northern Jordan

Jerash

Jerash pairs remarkable Roman-era monuments with scenic reservoirs, wooded hills and local town life.

IN THE PHOTO · King Talal Dam · Jerash

King Talal Dam lies across the Zarqa River in the hills of Jerash Governorate. Its broad reservoir curves between green ridges, offering striking viewpoints and an unexpected natural contrast to the ancient colonnades of nearby Jerash.

04 / 12 · Northeastern Jordan

Mafraq

Mafraq opens onto Jordan’s eastern Badia, with basalt archaeological towns and remote desert landscapes.

IN THE PHOTO · Burqu Castle · Eastern Mafraq

Burqu Castle stands in a remote desert setting beside the seasonal waters of Burqu. Its weathered stone walls and the surrounding rocky terrain reveal a quieter side of Jordan, shaped by historic travel routes, desert wildlife and wide open horizons.

05 / 12 · Central & Eastern Jordan

Zarqa

Zarqa connects busy urban neighbourhoods with the oasis landscapes and wildlife of the eastern desert.

IN THE PHOTO · Shaumari Wildlife Reserve · Azraq

Shaumari Wildlife Reserve near Azraq is an important conservation landscape for desert species, including the Arabian oryx pictured here. The reserve introduces visitors to wildlife protection and the fragile ecosystems of Jordan’s arid eastern plains.

06 / 12 · West-Central Jordan

Balqa

Balqa is home to historic As-Salt, green highlands, the Jordan Valley and Dead Sea landscapes.

IN THE PHOTO · Baptism Site · Jordan River

Bethany Beyond the Jordan, also known as Al-Maghtas, is a major pilgrimage and archaeological site beside the Jordan River. Its churches and historic remains reflect a long Christian tradition associated with the baptism of Jesus.

07 / 12 · Central Jordan

Amman

Amman blends ancient hilltop archaeology, vibrant neighbourhoods, modern culture and everyday city life.

IN THE PHOTO · King Abdullah I Mosque · Amman

The blue-domed King Abdullah I Mosque is one of Amman’s most recognisable modern landmarks. Its impressive dome, twin minarets and Islamic design create a distinctive sight in the capital’s urban landscape.

08 / 12 · Central Highlands

Madaba

Madaba is celebrated for Byzantine mosaics, religious heritage and striking landscapes above the Dead Sea.

IN THE PHOTO · Mukawir / Machaerus · Madaba

Mukawir, or ancient Machaerus, is a dramatic hilltop fortress site southwest of Madaba. The rugged ruins overlook the Dead Sea and are traditionally connected with the imprisonment and death of John the Baptist, making the place both historic and atmospheric.

09 / 12 · Southern Highlands

Karak

Karak offers imposing castles, traditional markets and deep valleys reaching toward the southern Dead Sea.

IN THE PHOTO · Lot’s Cave · Ghor as-Safi

Lot’s Cave is located on a hillside near Ghor as-Safi, south of the Dead Sea. Long associated with the biblical story of Lot, the site also preserves remains of a Byzantine church, offering a glimpse into centuries of pilgrimage and regional history.

Tafilah

Tafilah is a peaceful southern highland governorate with ancient sites, hot springs and Dana’s mountain trails.

IN THE PHOTO · Afra Hot Springs · Tafilah

Afra Hot Springs lies in a rocky valley in Tafilah, where warm mineral water cascades over stone terraces. The springs bring a surprising oasis-like setting to the surrounding rugged highlands, combining distinctive geology with a long local tradition of bathing.

11 / 12 · Southern Jordan

Ma'an

Ma’an is the gateway to Petra and a landscape of sandstone mountains, ancient trade routes and desert journeys.

IN THE PHOTO · Shobak Castle · Ma’an

Shobak Castle rises above the southern Jordanian hills on a remote rocky summit. Built during the Crusader period, its surviving walls and chambers reveal the strategic importance of this mountainous landscape and reward visitors with sweeping views.

Aqaba

Aqaba combines Red Sea beaches, colourful coral habitats, lively city streets and centuries of trade history.

IN THE PHOTO · Ancient Ayla · Aqaba

Ancient Ayla preserves the excavated stone foundations of an early Islamic coastal town in modern Aqaba. The ruins recall the city’s role in trade and travel along the Red Sea, just a short distance from today’s busy streets and waterfront.

GOVERNORATE 01 / 12 · THE CULTURAL NORTH

Irbid

Irbid is one of Jordan’s most culturally rich northern governorates, where university life, busy neighbourhoods and centuries of history meet. Its fertile highlands have long supported agriculture, especially olives, while archaeological sites reveal stories from the classical world and much earlier periods.

Start with the streets of Irbid, where cafés and local markets bring the city to life, then venture west to Umm Qais for impressive ancient ruins and views across the surrounding landscape. The green valleys of Wadi Rayyan and the countryside around Wadi Ziglab offer a quieter side of the governorate, with scenery that changes beautifully throughout the seasons.

Discover local highlights →
Ancient Gadara · Northwest Irbid

Umm Qais

Walk through ancient Gadara, where a Roman theatre and historic stone streets sit high above the northern valleys. Umm Qais brings together archaeology and striking views toward the Yarmouk Valley and Lake Tiberias, making the experience as scenic as it is historical.

Irbid city

Irbid City Centre

The modern Irbid City Centre complex shows a different side of northern Jordan: shopping, meeting friends and daily city life. It offers visitors a contemporary stop between museums, local cafés and the archaeological sites surrounding Irbid.

More to explore in Irbid

Green valleys · Irbid Governorate

Wadi Rayyan

Wadi Rayyan, also called Wadi al-Yabis, is celebrated for green hills, natural scenery and seasonal watercourses. Its landscape invites nature photography and countryside walks, especially when the surrounding slopes turn green after winter rains.

Jordan Valley slopes · Irbid Governorate

Wadi Ziglab

Winding roads cut through the hills around Wadi Ziglab, where olive groves and patchwork agricultural landscapes extend toward the Jordan Valley. The valley is a good illustration of the greener, quieter countryside beyond Irbid’s urban centre.

GOVERNORATE 02 / 12 · CASTLES & FORESTS

Ajloun

Ajloun is a wonderfully green part of northern Jordan, famous for its wooded mountains, historic villages and medieval castle. Its elevated landscapes offer a refreshing contrast to the country’s drier southern and eastern regions, especially during spring when hillsides turn bright green.

Discover the places ↓
GET TO KNOW AJLOUN

Ajloun Castle overlooks the surrounding countryside and recalls the strategic importance of these highlands in the Ayyubid period. Beyond the castle, visitors can wander through the town, enjoy woodland walks and ride the cable car for elevated views of the region. Ajloun rewards travellers who enjoy a mix of history, nature and local hospitality.

Ajloun’s landscape is shaped by forests, hillside villages and a long history of settlement. The area’s medieval castle is a famous landmark, but its quieter religious and archaeological sites, including Mar Elias, reveal more stories from the region’s past.

A day here can combine a castle visit with a cable-car ride, woodland scenery and time in downtown Ajloun. Spring is especially picturesque in the green highlands, although each season offers a different view of the hills.

Places to discover in Ajloun

Ajloun highlands

Ajloun Castle

Ajloun Castle, a medieval fortress built in the 12th century, occupies a commanding hilltop above forests and villages. Its thick walls, towers and stone chambers reflect a strategic defensive role, while the surrounding viewpoints reveal why this highland position mattered.

Ajloun town

Ajloun Downtown

Downtown Ajloun is an inviting introduction to everyday life in the northern highlands. Market stalls, local shops and neighbourhood streets provide opportunities to discover regional food, meet residents and experience the area beyond its famous castle and forests.

Wooded highlands · Ajloun

Ajloun Forests

Ajloun’s wooded slopes contain oak trees and other Mediterranean vegetation, creating some of northern Jordan’s most pleasant green scenery. The forests are particularly beautiful in spring, and their sheltered trails and viewpoints offer a welcome change from city streets.

Ajloun highlands

Ajloun Cable Car

The Ajloun cable car carries visitors above forested hills, revealing broad views across the governorate’s ridges and valleys. The elevated ride offers a memorable perspective on the countryside and a different way to appreciate Ajloun’s distinctive green terrain.

GOVERNORATE 03 / 12 · ANCIENT STREETS

Jerash

Jerash is celebrated for its exceptionally well-preserved Roman-era city, with grand columns, temples, theatres and public spaces that let visitors imagine life in antiquity. The modern governorate also includes welcoming communities, neighbourhood markets and countryside beyond the famous archaeological park.

Spend time among the monumental remains of ancient Gerasa, then explore the local market for a more contemporary perspective. For nature, head toward the forests of Dibeen, where wooded hills and walking routes offer fresh air and a beautiful contrast to the city’s golden stone. Jerash brings heritage and outdoor exploration close together.

Discover local highlights →
Ancient Gerasa · Jerash

The Ruins of Jerash

At ancient Gerasa, visitors can walk around the monumental Oval Plaza, pass through long colonnaded streets and discover impressive theatres and temples. The remarkably preserved stone architecture makes Jerash one of Jordan’s most important windows into urban life during the Roman era.

Jerash city centre

Jerash Market

Beyond the archaeological park, the streets of Jerash are filled with local shops and everyday activity. Visiting the market offers a chance to see the city as residents know it, from food stalls and small businesses to the social rhythm of the town.

More to explore in Jerash

Hover over a photo to learn more
Dibeen · Jerash Governorate

Dibeen Forest

Dibeen Forest is known for rolling hills and Mediterranean woodland landscapes. The blend of pines, oaks and other trees gives Jerash Governorate a wonderfully green character, offering natural views that complement the ancient ruins nearby.

Dibeen woodland area

Dibeen Trails

The wooded paths of Dibeen lead through green hills and rocky terrain, with peaceful forest views along the way. Visitors can enjoy the landscape on suitable walking routes while respecting protected natural areas and following local access guidance.

GOVERNORATE 04 / 12 · BASALT & HORIZONS

Mafraq

Mafraq stretches across an important meeting point of northern settlements, trade routes and the open landscapes of eastern Jordan. The governorate combines working towns and markets with remarkably atmospheric archaeological remains, including the black-basalt architecture of Umm al-Jimal.

Discover the places ↓
GET TO KNOW MAFRAQ

Beyond its urban centres, wide desert scenery shapes much of the region’s character. Visitors can explore the story of historical settlements and travel routes, see the extraordinary stones of Umm al-Jimal and discover the remote surroundings of Burqu. Because some desert locations are isolated, careful journey planning and appropriate local guidance are important.

The governorate preserves impressive basalt-built archaeological sites such as Umm al-Jimal and stretches far into the eastern Badia. This is a region where stone settlements, desert fortifications and seasonal water sources reveal how people adapted to an often demanding environment.

Visitors can explore the modern city of Mafraq before heading toward the distant landscapes of Burqu. Remote excursions are best planned with reliable transport, local knowledge and attention to changing weather and road conditions.

Places to discover in Mafraq

Northeast of Mafraq

Umm al-Jimal

Umm al-Jimal is renowned for its distinctive black basalt buildings, arches and stone streets. The ruins reveal layers of life in a historic settlement on the edge of the eastern Badia, where architecture and volcanic stone give the site its unforgettable character.

Mafraq city

The Eastern Badia

The eastern Badia surrounding Mafraq is known for open desert scenery, striking rock formations and historic travel routes.

Eastern Badia · Mafraq

Burqu Lake & Desert

The Burqu area brings together stony desert terrain, seasonal water and the remains of an old stone fortification. This remote landscape is especially memorable for the contrast between dry horizons and water after rain; visits require careful route planning.

Burqu area · Eastern Jordan

Burqu Desert Camp

A traditional outdoor gathering beside the water at Burqu captures the quiet appeal of Jordan’s eastern desert. Rugs, cushions and open views invite a slower experience focused on the surrounding landscape, while responsible visitors take care to leave the fragile environment undisturbed.

GOVERNORATE 05 / 12 · CITY & OASIS

Zarqa

Zarqa is a dynamic governorate that connects urban life in central Jordan with the oasis and desert environments farther east. Its busy city districts contrast with Azraq, where wetland habitats, historic forts and desert travel routes tell a different story of the region.

The Azraq area has long been significant as a source of water in an arid landscape, while its castle preserves layers of history. Visitors can combine cultural stops with birdwatching opportunities at the wetland reserve and broader views of the eastern desert. The governorate makes a compelling destination for travellers interested in both modern Jordan and its natural environment.

Discover local highlights →
Azraq · Zarqa Governorate

Azraq Castle

Azraq Castle is built largely from dark basalt stone and stands near an oasis that once attracted travellers crossing eastern Jordan. Its heavy stone walls, courtyard and defensive features reflect centuries of history at a strategically important point on desert routes.

Zarqa city

Azraq Oasis

Azraq Wetland Reserve protects an important oasis habitat in Zarqa Governorate, with walking trails and migratory birds.

Azraq oasis · Eastern Jordan

Azraq Wetland Reserve

A wooden boardwalk leads across pools and reeds at Azraq Wetland Reserve, an important refuge for migratory birds. Restoration work helps conserve part of a fragile oasis ecosystem; visitors can appreciate the contrast between open water and the surrounding dry eastern landscape.

Eastern Badia · Zarqa region

Eastern Desert Adventure

A guided journey through the eastern Badia reveals wide stony plains, remote roads and subtle desert colours. A suitable vehicle, experienced local guidance and respect for the landscape are important when exploring these exposed areas far from busy towns.

GOVERNORATE 06 / 12 · HIGHLANDS TO THE SEA

Balqa

Balqa is known for the historic city of As-Salt and a remarkable change of elevation from fertile highlands toward the shores of the Dead Sea. Its old neighbourhoods, stone houses and hospitable communities invite visitors to slow down and appreciate Jordanian urban heritage.

Discover the places ↓
GET TO KNOW BALQA

Explore the heritage streets and markets of Salt, then continue toward scenic valleys and the extraordinary mineral-rich waters of the Dead Sea. The landscape offers a rewarding combination of culture, mountains and lowland scenery. This collection also features nearby Iraq al-Amir, which is administratively in Amman Governorate and makes a convenient excursion in the wider west-central Jordan region.

As-Salt is a highlight of Balqa, famous for golden-stone architecture, lively streets and long traditions of community life. Beyond the city, the hills descend toward the Jordan Valley and the unusual mineral-rich environment of the Dead Sea.

The governorate also includes significant religious and natural attractions, including the Baptism Site near the Jordan River and the valley landscapes of Wadi Shuaib. Together they make Balqa an appealing journey through history, culture and changing terrain.

Nearby excursion · Amman Governorate

Iraq al-Amir

Iraq al-Amir, west of Amman, is known for Qasr al-Abd, a striking ancient stone monument surrounded by a green valley. Although this nearby excursion is in Amman Governorate, it can complement a wider visit to the heritage towns and landscapes of Balqa.

As-Salt · Balqa Governorate

Old Salt Markets

The historic streets of As-Salt are lined with distinctive golden-stone buildings and traditional shops. Wandering the market reveals the town’s layered urban heritage and a welcoming daily rhythm, with food stalls, small businesses and old neighbourhood lanes.

Western Balqa shore

The Dead Sea

The Dead Sea is famous for its extraordinary salinity and the pale mineral formations along its shores. Against a dramatic backdrop of desert hills, the vivid colours of the water make this one of Jordan’s most recognisable natural landscapes.

Balqa highlands

Wadi Shuaib

Wadi Shuaib stretches through the hills and valleys around As-Salt, linking green pockets of countryside with local settlements. Its changing scenery, vegetation and historic associations make the valley an interesting quieter excursion within Balqa.

GOVERNORATE 07 / 12 · THE CAPITAL

Amman

Amman, Jordan’s capital governorate, is a city of remarkable contrasts. Its hillside neighbourhoods, archaeological landmarks, cafés, cultural spaces and modern commercial centres reveal how ancient and contemporary life coexist. The Citadel tells stories of earlier civilisations, while downtown streets remain full of activity.

Visitors can explore historical viewpoints, contemporary shopping districts and greener places for a break from city life. Amman also serves as a useful base for wider trips across Jordan. Whether your interests are architecture, food, local markets or outdoor activities, the governorate offers many different ways to experience the country.

Jabal al-Qal’a · Amman

Amman Citadel

Amman Citadel overlooks the capital from Jabal al-Qal’a and preserves evidence of different eras of settlement. Classical columns, archaeological remains and wide city views combine to tell the story of a place where ancient and modern Amman meet.

Amman shopping districts

Modern Amman

The City Mall shopping complex represents contemporary urban life in Amman, with modern retail, cafés and indoor gathering spaces. It offers a familiar city experience alongside the capital’s older markets, museums and historic neighbourhoods.

Amman Governorate

Amman National Park

Amman National Park offers wooded scenery, rocky open spaces and a quieter atmosphere than the surrounding urban districts. Green areas provide opportunities for relaxed recreation and show that the capital’s wider landscapes are not only busy streets and buildings.

Amman-area climbing locations

Amman National Park

A wooded retreat near the capital, with spaces for outdoor walks and family outings.

GOVERNORATE 08 / 12 · MOSAICS & VIEWS

Madaba

Madaba is internationally known for its exceptional mosaics, religious heritage and scenic highland setting. The town’s historic churches and craft traditions make it a rewarding destination for people interested in art, archaeology and the layered history of the region.

Discover the places ↓
GET TO KNOW MADABA

Beyond the streets of Madaba, Mount Nebo offers wide views toward the Jordan Valley, while the landscapes around Ma’in and Wadi Hidan introduce water, steep valleys and rocky scenery. Together, these attractions create a distinctive mix of cultural discovery and outdoor adventure, often within an easy journey from Amman.

Madaba is known as the City of Mosaics, but its attractions extend beyond its churches and famous historical map. Mount Nebo, hilltop ruins, warm mineral springs and dramatic wadis tell stories of faith, nature and ancient travel through the region.

Spend time in the local markets, enjoy elevated views toward the Jordan Valley and visit the remains of Mukawir. The governorate offers a memorable balance of archaeology, religious heritage and outdoor scenery.

Places to discover in Madaba

Hover over a photo to discover its story
West of Madaba

Mount Nebo

Mount Nebo is an important religious heritage site with sweeping views toward the Jordan Valley and the Dead Sea. The sanctuary, associated biblical traditions and historic mosaics make it a meaningful stop for visitors interested in history and landscape together.

Madaba city

Madaba Town & Markets

Madaba’s markets and neighbourhood streets reveal the everyday life behind the city’s celebrated mosaics. Shops, local crafts and traditional foods invite travellers to explore slowly and experience the city’s warm community character between its historic sites.

Ma’in · Madaba Governorate

Ma’in Hot Springs

At Ma’in Hot Springs, mineral-rich warm water flows down cliffs into a dramatic valley landscape. The waterfalls and rocky surroundings create one of Madaba Governorate’s most recognisable natural attractions, known for relaxing bathing experiences in designated facilities.

Madaba Governorate

Wadi Hidan

Wadi Hidan is a scenic canyon carved through dark rock, where flowing water and rugged walls create opportunities for adventure. Conditions can change with the seasons; canyon excursions are best undertaken with experienced guides and appropriate safety planning.

GOVERNORATE 09 / 12 · FORTRESS & CANYONS

Karak

Karak is a governorate of steep highlands, strong historical identity and remarkable natural scenery. Its famous castle stands above the city as a reminder of medieval routes and military history, while the surrounding countryside descends toward striking valleys and canyons.

A visit to Karak can begin with the castle’s stone corridors and viewpoints, continue through the city’s market streets and finish among the waters and cliffs of Wadi Bin Hammad or the dramatic landscapes of Wadi Mujib. These destinations show the remarkable variety within one governorate, from built heritage to adventure-filled nature.

Discover local highlights →
Karak city

Karak Castle

Karak Castle dominates the city from a high ridge. The medieval fortress contains imposing walls, halls and passages that reflect centuries of conflict and strategic importance, while its elevated position offers excellent views over the rugged highlands.

Karak city centre

Karak Old Market

Karak’s traditional shopping streets bring local life into focus beneath the famous fortress. Market stalls and neighbourhood businesses give visitors a sense of the city’s everyday culture and provide a contrast to the monumental stone architecture above.

More to explore in Karak

Hover over a photo to learn more
Karak Governorate

Wadi Bin Hammad

Wadi Bin Hammad is a narrow canyon where water, ferns and hanging vegetation create an unexpected green oasis among rocky cliffs. The combination of flowing streams and dramatic canyon walls makes the valley memorable, although access depends on safe weather and water conditions.

Near the Dead Sea · Karak area

Wadi Mujib

Wadi Mujib is famous for deep canyon walls and adventurous water routes descending toward the Dead Sea. Its striking rock formations and flowing water create a dramatic natural setting; seasonal access and safety guidance are particularly important here.

GOVERNORATE 10 / 12 · MOUNTAINS & TRAILS

Tafilah

Tafilah is a peaceful highland governorate where dramatic rock formations, historic settlements and protected natural areas define the journey. Its rugged terrain and quieter roads offer a strong sense of the geography and rural traditions of southern Jordan.

Discover the places ↓
GET TO KNOW TAFILAH

Visitors can discover the archaeological landscape around Sela, experience the town’s everyday markets and journey toward the diverse environments of Dana Biosphere Reserve. The trails toward Feynan take travellers through changing elevations and beautiful natural scenery. Because routes vary in difficulty, it is best to plan hikes using current local information and responsible guiding services.

Tafilah is a place of rugged mountains and surprising natural contrasts. The rocky plateau around Sela recalls ancient settlement, while nearby warm springs and the landscapes of Dana invite visitors to discover different sides of southern Jordan.

Hikers can enjoy broad views on routes toward Feynan, while quieter town markets offer a look at everyday life. The governorate is particularly rewarding for travellers who prefer nature, archaeology and a slower pace.

Tafilah Governorate

Sela Archaeological Site

The rock-top archaeological landscape of Sela rises above rugged terrain in Tafilah. Its isolated setting, old stone remains and commanding views illustrate how people used the region’s natural cliffs for settlement and defense throughout history.

Tafilah town

Dana Landscapes

Dana Biosphere Reserve is known for dramatic mountain landscapes and trails in Tafilah Governorate.

Dana · Tafilah Governorate

Dana Biosphere Reserve

Dana Biosphere Reserve protects a remarkable landscape of mountains, valleys and distinct ecological zones. Its scenic viewpoints and varied terrain make it an important destination for nature enthusiasts who want to appreciate Jordan’s environmental diversity.

Dana to Feynan region

Dana–Feynan Trails

The Dana–Feynan area offers rocky hills, deep valleys and extensive walking routes connecting highland and lower-elevation landscapes. These paths showcase dramatic geology and changing natural habitats; longer hikes require suitable preparation and guidance.

GOVERNORATE 11 / 12 · THE ROSE-RED SOUTH

Ma'an

Ma'an is home to Petra, one of the world’s most recognisable archaeological destinations, and to landscapes shaped by sandstone mountains, ancient trade routes and the communities of southern Jordan. For centuries, travellers and merchants moved through this region, leaving behind an extraordinary cultural legacy.

Explore Petra’s famous carved monuments, visit the smaller archaeological setting of Little Petra and discover walking routes that reveal viewpoints away from the busiest paths. Wadi Musa provides the modern gateway to these attractions. For a wider southern Jordan itinerary, the gallery also points to Wadi Rum, which lies in Aqaba Governorate rather than Ma'an, and is a separate destination worth planning carefully.

Discover local highlights →
Wadi Musa · Ma’an Governorate

Petra

Petra’s Treasury appears through the towering sandstone cliffs of the Siq, creating one of the most striking entrances in the ancient world. Beyond this famous facade, visitors discover Nabataean tombs, carved temples, trails and a vast archaeological landscape.

Gateway town to Petra · Ma’an

Wadi Musa

Colourful souvenir stalls near the Petra visitor routes offer handicrafts, textiles and mementos framed by dramatic sandstone. Wadi Musa is the modern gateway town to Petra, providing travellers with accommodation, shops and services before and after exploring the archaeological site.

Siq al-Barid · Ma’an

Little Petra

Little Petra, also called Siq al-Barid, contains narrow sandstone passages, carved niches and building facades. Its quieter scale offers additional insight into the Nabataean world and makes it a rewarding nearby visit alongside the larger archaeological city of Petra.

Petra area · Ma’an

Little Petra

A Nabataean archaeological site near Petra, known for its narrow canyon and rock-carved facades.

Aqaba Governorate · Southern Jordan

Wadi Rum · Nearby Excursion

Camel riding against sandstone mountains evokes the traditional desert journeys of Wadi Rum. This famous landscape belongs to Aqaba Governorate; it is shown here as a nearby excursion that can complement a visit to Petra and Ma’an’s southern attractions.

GOVERNORATE 12 / 12 · JORDAN BY THE SEA

Aqaba

Aqaba is Jordan’s gateway to the Red Sea, where warm weather, mountain views and a vibrant waterfront create a very different atmosphere from the country’s inland cities. The governorate combines maritime history, commercial activity and a coastline admired for its clear water and marine life.

Discover the places ↓
GET TO KNOW AQABA

Visitors can explore the city’s historical sites and markets, relax beside the water and discover coral reefs through responsible snorkelling and diving. Aqaba also offers convenient access to nearby southern desert scenery, making it a natural final destination for a journey through Jordan’s varied landscapes.

Aqaba’s location on the Gulf of Aqaba gives it a distinctive character: desert mountains behind the city, marine life beneath the water and historic trade routes along the coast. Its archaeological sites include the remains of ancient Ayla and the stone walls of Aqaba Castle.

Visitors can alternate between the city centre, seaside walks and responsibly guided diving or snorkelling. Colourful coral habitats and underwater attractions make the gulf especially appealing to travellers interested in marine nature.

Places to discover in Aqaba

Hover over a photo to discover its story
Aqaba waterfront

Aqaba Castle

Aqaba Castle, also called the Mamluk Fort, stands close to the waterfront and reflects the city’s long strategic and maritime history. Its sturdy stone facade and historic associations form a fascinating contrast with the nearby modern port and seaside districts.

Aqaba

Aqaba City Center

The Aqaba City Center complex shows the modern shopping side of Jordan’s southern coastal city. Visitors can explore stores and everyday urban life before continuing to the beach, historic monuments and the Red Sea’s marine attractions.

Gulf of Aqaba · Dive sites

Aqaba Underwater Attractions

A submerged military vehicle provides a striking sight at one of Aqaba’s underwater diving attractions. Artificial underwater exhibits and marine habitats add interest to guided dives, while responsible practices help protect the Red Sea’s delicate coral ecosystems.

Gulf of Aqaba · Aqaba

Red Sea Diving

A scuba diver swims among bright fish and coral gardens in the clear waters of the Gulf of Aqaba. The northern Red Sea offers remarkable underwater biodiversity, best enjoyed with qualified operators and care to avoid touching or damaging marine life.
